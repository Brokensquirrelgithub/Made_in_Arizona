# Made in Arizona — story and character bible

## The promise

**117° Auto Care is a repair shop that achieves repairs through superior firepower.**

People bring Johnny ordinary automotive problems. Those problems have a habit of escaping into the desert, joining a private security detail, hiding the useful parts, or becoming evidence in a larger crime. Johnny diagnoses the job from the garage. Stallion goes into the field and engineers a solution. Suzuki handles the paperwork.

The comedy comes from treating impossible vehicle combat as honest repair work. The characters never call themselves mercenaries, bounty hunters or vigilantes. Every mission is a work order. Every target has a mechanical fault. Every explosion is a diagnostic result.

## Central cast

### Stallion — field mechanic and player character

Stallion is a Black man in his early thirties with a shaped Afro, sunglasses and clean, practical workwear. He is new to 117° Auto Care, but not new to machines or danger. Johnny calls him the shop's runner; Stallion thinks of himself as the person who finishes the job.

He is composed, observant and extremely capable. He does not explain a plan when he can demonstrate it. His lines are short, literal and final. The humor comes from how calmly he accepts Johnny's absurd definition of automotive repair.

Dialogue rules:

- Usually one sentence, often fewer than six words.
- Never boasts or narrates his own coolness.
- Talks in diagnoses: “Bad bearing.” “Found the leak.” “Needs alignment.”
- Solves problems physically, then reports the result.
- His rare longer line should mark a genuine moral decision.

### Johnny — proprietor and mechanic

Johnny runs 117° Auto Care. He looks and acts like a working mechanic: stained shop shirt, name patch, heavy boots, pencil behind one ear, hands that never quite lose the grease. He understands engines, customers and Arizona bureaucracy in that order.

Johnny is the dispatcher, mentor and comic voice on the radio. He describes armed convoys as warranty complications and fortifications as difficult fasteners. Beneath the jokes, he believes a repair shop owes its neighborhood practical help.

Dialogue rules:

- Uses real mechanical language wherever possible.
- Explains objectives as repair diagnoses.
- Complains about bad workmanship more than incoming gunfire.
- Never sounds slick; he sounds experienced, tired and useful.
- When the stakes become serious, the joke drops away before Stallion acts.

### Suzuki — garage dog and silent administrator

Suzuki is always a dog and never speaks words. She behaves like a dog until she does one perfectly ordinary human office task: stamping a form, filing an invoice, carrying a key ring, putting a paw on a scanner or moving a clipboard into Johnny's reach.

Nobody explains this. Johnny treats her work as routine. Stallion acknowledges it with the same calm he gives everything else.

## Opening work order

### 1. First Day, Final Notice

Purpose: establish the garage, the player's role, the game's definition of “repair,” the three-person dynamic and the first thread of the Sunsprawl plot.

#### Garage briefing

Johnny is under a shop car with only his boots visible. Stallion arrives for his first shift. Suzuki sits at the service counter beside a stack of work orders.

Johnny: “You Stallion?”

Stallion: “That's me.”

Johnny rolls out, looks at Stallion's sunglasses, then at the dark garage.

Johnny: “Prescription?”

Stallion: “No.”

Johnny: “Good. Means you can read a road in direct sunlight.”

A battered loaner tears away from the garage. Its front wheel wobbles violently.

Johnny: “That was our customer.”

Stallion: “Was?”

Johnny: “Stopped being one when he stole the loaner. Welcome to 117° Auto Care. We fix cars.”

Stallion watches the car disappear into the desert.

Stallion: “And when the problem isn't the car?”

Johnny hands him the keys to an armed shop vehicle.

Johnny: “Then you take the shop car and fix the situation.”

Suzuki stamps the abandoned invoice **PAST DUE**.

#### Stage 1 — road test

Objective: pursue the stolen loaner to Half-Price Horizon Salvage.

Johnny: “Front wheel's attached with a hose clamp. Don't let him exceed the clamp's rated speed.”

Stallion: “It has a rated speed?”

Johnny: “We're about to establish one.”

The chase teaches steering, boost and the objective marker.

#### Stage 2 — corrective service

The loaner enters an armed salvage yard.

Johnny: “There's our loaner. Customer replaced the wheel nut with a hose clamp.”

Stallion: “I'll correct the workmanship.”

Johnny: “Clear the yard first. Propane tanks are persuasive shop tools.”

Objective: destroy three hostile vehicles. The fight teaches weapons, environmental explosions, repairs and pickups.

#### Stage 3 — paperwork

Objective: recover the finance ledger.

Johnny: “That's no customer ledger. Sunsprawl is buying cooling plants, roads and vehicle debt. A bad alternator we can replace. A business model like that needs removing.”

Stallion picks up the ledger.

Stallion: “Found the problem.”

Johnny: “Bring it home. I want to know who sends armed trucks over an invoice smaller than a set of brake pads.”

#### Stage 4 — return to the shop

Objective: escape the response crew and reach 117° Auto Care.

Johnny: “Shop door's open. Try not to bring the whole junkyard.”

Stallion: “No promises.”

#### Completion

Johnny examines the recovered ledger. Suzuki sits beside the invoice tray wearing a green accounting visor that no one saw her put on.

Johnny: “First day complete. Customer owes one hundred seventy-three dollars, a wheel bearing and one apology.”

Stallion: “Put it on the invoice.”

Johnny: “Suzuki already filed it under ‘unlikely.’”

Suzuki slides the stamped invoice into a cabinet, closes the drawer and looks at the next work order.

## Campaign dialogue pattern

Every later work order should carry the same four beats:

1. **Diagnosis:** Johnny explains the customer problem and the practical stakes.
2. **Method:** Stallion identifies the shortest physical solution.
3. **Complication:** A customer, local contact or antagonist reveals how the job connects to Sunsprawl.
4. **Shop close:** Johnny frames the result as completed service; Suzuki performs the final silent administrative gag.

Stallion remains the only playable protagonist. Ellis, Bea, Silt, Nova, Ivo and Rick can stay in the world as customers, specialists and local contacts, but they are not selectable drivers.
